# Model card: Market regime detection

- Generated by `python scripts/run_ml.py` (seed 42); every figure below is read from that run's stored results.
- Data snapshot id: `2dca6b196267` (prices 2021-10-06 to 2026-10-06, 25 companies)

## Purpose

Describe the last five years of the Nifty 50 as alternating calm and turbulent periods, and show
how volatility and correlation differed between them. **Descriptive segmentation only.**

## Data

1,221 daily Nifty 50 returns, and company returns on the same dates.

## Method

2-state Markov switching, switching variance, constant mean, fitted by maximum likelihood (statsmodels). Each day gets a smoothed probability
of being in the high-variance state; a day is labelled turbulent when that probability exceeds 0.5.
As a transparent comparison, a rule labels a day turbulent when trailing 21-day volatility above its 75% quantile.

## Validation

There is no ground truth for a regime, so there is no accuracy to report and no baseline to beat.
The model is checked for convergence (converged)
and compared with the threshold rule: the two agree on 85.2%
of days.

## Results

Estimated annualized volatility: 10.2% in the calm state and
22.4% in the turbulent state. Expected duration:
45 trading days calm,
13 turbulent. 18.9%
of days are labelled turbulent. Log-likelihood -1463.1, AIC 2938.2.

**By Markov-switching regime**

| Regime | Days | Share of days | Nifty 50 volatility | Mean stock volatility | Mean pairwise correlation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| calm | 990 | 81.1% | 10.1% | 21.6% | 0.171 |
| turbulent | 231 | 18.9% | 23.9% | 30.4% | 0.411 |

**By threshold rule**

| Regime | Days | Share of days | Nifty 50 volatility | Mean stock volatility | Mean pairwise correlation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| calm | 901 | 73.8% | 11.0% | 21.8% | 0.191 |
| turbulent | 300 | 24.6% | 20.1% | 27.7% | 0.360 |

## Limitations

- **Smoothed probabilities use the whole sample.** A day's label depends on what happened
  afterwards, so the labels could not have been known at the time.
- **Two states are assumed**, not found. The data were not asked whether three would fit better.
- **Higher correlation in the turbulent state is partly mechanical:** correlation estimates rise
  with volatility even when the underlying relationship is unchanged.
- **One index, five years.** Few turbulent episodes are in the sample.

## What this must not be used for

- **It is not a trading or timing signal.** The labels are fitted with hindsight.
- Predicting when the next turbulent period will start or end.
- Selecting, recommending or ranking securities. FinSight is an analytics tool, not investment advice.
